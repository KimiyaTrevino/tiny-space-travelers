## 🔬 What will we explore?

We’ll compare two yeast strains:

- **Wild type:** yeast with the RAD51 gene present.
- **rad51Δ:** yeast missing RAD51, a gene involved in repairing DNA damage.

Using measurements prepared from **NASA’s OSD-964 dataset**, we’ll explore how their light-absorbance readings change over time and across radiation treatments.

These experiments took place **on Earth**, supporting research relevant to space exploration.

## 💻 What will you learn?

- Load and preview a data table.
- Understand its columns.
- Select samples by radiation dose.
- Calculate average readings.
- Create and interpret graphs.
- Explain what the evidence can and cannot tell us.

## 💡 Remember

**Radiation is the treatment. Light is the measurement method.**

Radiation dose is recorded in **grays (Gy)**. Absorbance readings measure light at **570 and 600 nanometers**, using yeast samples and AlamarBlue dye.

Our graphs explore raw light measurements. They do not directly count surviving cells or measure DNA repair.

## 🌌 Why tiny space travelers?

Yeast gives researchers a manageable way to investigate how living cells respond to radiation. This workshop is a first step toward exploring the connections between biology, computing, biomedical engineering, and astrobiology.

Bring your curiosity—and let’s investigate!
